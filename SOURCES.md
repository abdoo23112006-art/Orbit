# Challenge sources

Reviewed on 14 November 2026. All five resources below are listed on the official challenge’s Resources tab. The app presents links and concise explanations, not copied datasets or live astronaut records. All example readings are synthetic.

Challenge: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/?tab=resources

| Resource | Use in Orbit |
| --- | --- |
| [NASA — 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/) | Organizes the field guide: radiation, isolation, distance from Earth, gravity fields, hostile/closed environments. |
| [NASA — Risks of Human Spaceflight](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/) | Explains why circulation, activity, immunity and behavioral well-being warrant attention. Vitals do not diagnose these risks. |
| [NASA — Ongoing HRP Research](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/) | Standard Measures and ARCHeR motivate consistent check-ins covering physiological indicators, activity, sleep and well-being. |
| [CSA — Monitoring Radiation for Astronaut Health](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp) | Explains that radiation exposure requires dedicated instrumentation such as CANS. No radiation dose is fabricated or inferred. |
| [CSA — Why Do We Conduct Science Experiments in Space?](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp) | Provides context for longer missions, communication delays and operational independence; motivates local journaling. |

The challenge suggests blood pressure, pulse, blood sugar, temperature, sleep hours and mood. Orbit implements those inputs. Numerical targets, the 1–5 mood scale, mission scenario, profile, routine and sample values are prototype design choices, not validated clinical instruments or NASA prescriptions. No NASA or CSA endorsement is implied.
